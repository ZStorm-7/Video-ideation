# Title patterns

≤ 60 characters (never past ~70: it gets cut), main keyword early, a curiosity gap the video truly pays off. The title,
the thumbnail and the first spoken line should all share the same key word.

| Pattern | Example (tech) | Example (another niche) |
|---|---|---|
| I [did X] for [time/constraint] | I Coded Only on My Phone for 7 Days | I Ate Only Gas-Station Food for a Week |
| Why [surprising fact] | Why Your Wi-Fi Is Slower Upstairs | Why Restaurant Eggs Taste Better |
| The [adjective] Truth About [thing] | The Uncomfortable Truth About Free VPNs | The Uncomfortable Truth About Protein Bars |
| [Number] [things] That [result] | 5 Settings That Doubled My Battery | 5 Fees That Cost Me $2,000 a Year |
| Stop [common habit] | Stop Closing Your Apps | Stop Stretching Before You Run |
| [A] vs [B]: [stakes] | $300 vs $3,000 Laptop: Same Code | $8 vs $90 Serum: One Month, One Face |
| How [person/thing] [did something unexpected] | How One Typo Crashed the Internet | How One Bakery Outlasted Three Chains |
| This [thing] [does something wrong/strange] | This Bug Cost $440 Million | This Fee Is Hidden in Every Flight |
| What Happens When You [everyday action] | What Happens When You Press Enter | What Happens When You Skip Breakfast for 30 Days |
| You've Been [doing X] Wrong | You've Been Charging Your Phone Wrong | You've Been Cooking Rice Wrong |

Write titles before the video (packaging first). Make 3; at least one uses a real autocomplete phrase (search), one is
pure curiosity (browse/Shorts feed). The title must be true to the video: a title the video can't pay off costs you the
next click, even if it wins this one. Translate the *pattern*, not the words: a title that sounds natural in the
audience's language beats a literal translation.
